## Key Question 1: Age range for Coast Guard active-duty enlistment

### Takeaway
Federal law permits original enlistment from 17 through 42 (10 U.S.C. 505(a)); the Coast Guard's own public figure for Enlisted Active Duty is 17-41 (Reserve 17-40), as shown on the JavaScript-rendered gocoastguard.com selector and echoed by several third-party sites. Older third-party pages still show 17-31 and should be treated as outdated.

### Cited Findings
- Statutory ceiling: 10 U.S.C. 505(a) allows original enlistments of "qualified, effective, and able-bodied persons who are not less than seventeen years of age" and "nor more than forty-two years of age"; the 2006 amendment (Pub. L. 109-163) "raised the maximum age in subsection (a) from thirty-five to forty-two." — [Cornell LII, 10 U.S.C. 505](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/505)
- The gocoastguard.com eligibility page is JavaScript-rendered; a plain fetch returned only general sections (fitness, tattoos, swim, waivers) and none of the program-specific age/education/AFQT content. The task assignment reports the live page shows 17-41 when "Enlisted: Active Duty" is selected; this could not be confirmed from the static HTML in this environment. — [gocoastguard.com eligibility requirements](https://www.gocoastguard.com/get-started/eligibility-requirements) (note: the shorter URL /eligibility-requirements returns 404)
- Third-party confirmation of 17-41: search results summarized "Coast Guard Age Requirements Active Duty 17-41 Reserve 17-40" and "Coast Guard: 17 - 41", with minimum age 17 "with parental consent", and Reserve maximum 40. — [usmilitary.com age requirements by branch](https://usmilitary.com/age-requirements-for-military-branches/); [operationmilitarykids.org Military Age Limits for 2026](https://www.operationmilitarykids.org/?p=21467) (search-snippet level; the usmilitary.com Coast Guard age article itself returned 403)
- ALCOAST 466/23 (22 Nov 2023), promulgating COMDTINST 1100.2H, lists among the changes "Updated age criteria for various programs and active duty enlistment" (specific numbers not in the bulletin text). — [ALCOAST 466/23, govdelivery](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCG/bulletins/37c4ff7)
- Outdated/conflicting third-party figures: military.com (page stamped "Updated on Aug 20, 2026") says enlisted applicants must "Be between 17-42 years old" (this is the statutory ceiling, not the Coast Guard's stated 41). — [military.com Join the Coast Guard](https://www.military.com/join-armed-forces/join-the-coast-guard); operationmilitarykids.org (page stamped "Updated February 22, 2026") still says "Between the ages of 17-31*" with reservists 17-40. — [operationmilitarykids.org Coast Guard requirements](https://www.operationmilitarykids.org/coast-guard-requirements/); militaryspot.com also shows 17-31 active / 17-40 reserve. — [militaryspot.com](https://www.militaryspot.com/coast-guard/requirements-for-the-coast-guard)

### Inferences
- The 17-31 figure that persists on several third-party pages reflects the pre-2023 Coast Guard limit; the statute has allowed up to 42 since 2006, so the service limit (41) is a service policy choice below the statutory ceiling, and the 2023 recruiting instruction (1100.2H) is the most likely point at which the age criteria moved.
- For a study tool, 17-41 (active duty) is the figure to present, with a "confirm with a recruiter" caveat and a note that the number is set by Coast Guard policy, not statute.

### Gaps
- Could not read the rendered gocoastguard.com selector text or COMDTINST 1100.2H/1100.2I (media.defense.gov 403) to quote the official 17-41 limit verbatim or to pin the date the limit rose from 31.

## Key Question 2: Parental consent at 17

### Takeaway
10 U.S.C. 505(a) requires written consent of a parent or guardian for any original enlistee under 18 who has a parent or guardian entitled to custody and control; the consent is recorded on the enlistment paperwork (DD Form 1966 / DD Form 4).

### Cited Findings
- Statute text (Cornell LII rendering of 10 U.S.C. 505(a)): "no person under eighteen years of age may be originally enlisted without the written consent of his parent or guardian" ... "if he has a parent or guardian entitled to his custody and control." — [Cornell LII, 10 U.S.C. 505](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/505)
- Amendment history on the same page: originally enacted by Pub. L. 90-235, effective January 2, 1968; later amended 1996 (Pub. L. 104-201), 2006 (Pub. L. 109-163, max age 35 to 42), 2008 (Pub. L. 110-417, enlistment period six to eight years), 2021 (Pub. L. 116-283, added Regular Space Force), 2023 (Pub. L. 118-31, removed Regular Space Force from list and added subsection (e) pointing to sections 20301 and 20302). — [Cornell LII, 10 U.S.C. 505](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/505)
- Third-party restatement: minimum age to join the Coast Guard is 17 "with parental consent." — [usmilitary.com age requirements](https://usmilitary.com/age-requirements-for-military-branches/) (search-snippet level)

### Inferences
- Because the Coast Guard is an armed force covered by 10 U.S.C. 505 (the section applies to "the Secretary concerned"), the parental-consent rule applies to Coast Guard enlistment regardless of its DHS parentage. The DD Form 1966 (Record of Military Processing) and DD Form 4 (Enlistment/Reenlistment Document) carry the parental/guardian consent section; this is standard DoD/MEPS practice but I did not retrieve the form text in this research (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- uscode.house.gov page for section 505 returned 404 at the URL tried; the quote above is from Cornell LII only.
- Did not retrieve the DD Form 1966 or DD Form 4 text showing the consent block; cite as general knowledge or verify separately.

## Key Question 3: Minimum AFQT by education tier, and history of the minimum

### Takeaway
Current (2025-2026) Coast Guard minimum AFQT is 32 for Tier 1 (high school diploma) applicants; Tier 2 (GED/alternative credential) is higher, reported as 47 by a site citing COMDTINST 1100.2I section 4.B.7 and as 50 by another third-party site; the official instruction could not be read to settle this. The historical minimum was 40 (highest of any service), and third-party sources say it fell to 36 and then to 32 in November 2023 with the new recruiting instruction, but no ALCOAST/news release confirming the lowering was found.

### Cited Findings
- Diploma minimum 32: "The minimum AFQT score on the ASVAB for enlisting in the U.S. Coast Guard is 32." — [military.com Join the Coast Guard (updated Aug 20, 2026)](https://www.military.com/join-armed-forces/join-the-coast-guard)
- Diploma minimum 32 and Tier 2 = 47, citing the instruction: "most Tier I prospects, meaning high school graduates, qualify to apply at 32 or more" and "Tier II prospects, alternative credential holders, at 47 or more", source given as "Section 4.B.7 of Coast Guard Recruiting, COMDTINST 1100.2I, dated June 2025"; the page says gocoastguard.com also lists 32, and that "A qualifying AFQT lets you apply; it is not an offer." — [test-ninjas.com Coast Guard jobs by ASVAB score](https://test-ninjas.com/coast-guard-jobs-by-asvab-score) (third party; says it checked figures Oct 2026)
- Tier II detail from the same source family: 1100.2I reportedly requires the Accessions Division to approve every Tier II enlistment; and "One completed semester, at least 15 semester hours or 20 quarter hours of college-level credit, is a Tier I credential" (citing 1100.2I sec. 4.C.3 and Table 4-5), so a GED holder with that credit is processed as Tier I. The Coast Guard table reportedly places an online distance-learning diploma in Tier II. — [test-ninjas.com Enlisting with a GED](https://test-ninjas.com/enlisting-with-a-ged-asvab) (third party)
- Conflicting GED figure (50): "The Coast Guard requires an AFQT of at least 50 with a GED"; same page: "The Coast Guard requires AFQT 32 with a high school diploma." — [asvab-hero.pages.dev Coast Guard ASVAB score](https://asvab-hero.pages.dev/coast-guard-asvab-score) (third party; contradicted on the GED figure by [test-ninjas.com](https://test-ninjas.com/coast-guard-jobs-by-asvab-score))
- History claim: "The Coast Guard historically required an AFQT of 40 for high school diploma holders, the highest minimum of any branch," then it "dropped to 36, then further to 32 in November 2023 when the CG updated its recruiting instruction (COMDTINST 1100.2)"; FAQ: "It was 40 until November 2023." No date given for the drop to 36. — [asvab-hero.pages.dev](https://asvab-hero.pages.dev/coast-guard-asvab-score) (third party, unverified against the instruction)
- Older third-party figures still in circulation: a recruiting-manual "decoder" page paraphrases the AFQT minimum as 40, "the highest across services", while citing 1100.2I only at chapter level and marking it "Snippet verified." — [honestmos.com recruiting manual decoder](https://www.honestmos.com/tools/recruiting-manual-decoder); web-search snippets (no specific page captured) also surfaced third-party pages still listing 36-40 for diploma holders and a GED figure of "47 and 15 college credit hours"; treat as unverified and undated, e.g. [uniontestprep.com minimum AFQT by branch](https://uniontestprep.com/asvab/blog/minimum-asvab-afqt-score-for-each-branch-of-the-military) and [operationmilitarykids.org ASVAB scores for Coast Guard jobs](https://www.operationmilitarykids.org/asvab-scores-for-coast-guard-jobs/) appeared in those results but were not fetched.
- Official document trail (titles/dates only, PDFs blocked): COMDTINST 1100.2H, "Coast Guard Recruiting," published Nov 22, 2023 at media.defense.gov (403 from here). — [CI_1100_2H.PDF](https://media.defense.gov/2023/Nov/22/2003345749/-1/-1/0/CI_1100_2H.PDF); ALCOAST 466/23 (22 Nov 2023) promulgated 1100.2H and cancelled the Coast Guard Recruiting Manual, COMDTINST M1100.2G; it lists two-year enlistments for non-prior-service recruits, updated age criteria, and updated Basic Financial Criteria (Table 4-7), but does not mention AFQT changes. — [ALCOAST 466/23](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCG/bulletins/37c4ff7); ALCOAST 251/25 (4 Jun 2025) cancelled 1100.2H and replaced it with COMDTINST 1100.2I, described as aligning with recent Executive Orders plus minor Direct Commission Cyber Officer changes; no mention of AFQT. — [ALCOAST 251/25](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCG/bulletins/3e39912)
- Older operationmilitarykids page (updated Feb 22, 2026) says "Received a high school diploma or GED equivalent" and that the Coast Guard "limits the number of Tier 2 (GED) applicants it accepts each year"; it gives no AFQT number. — [operationmilitarykids.org Coast Guard requirements](https://www.operationmilitarykids.org/coast-guard-requirements/)
- Historical official reference: the 2016 ASVAB Process Guide references ALCOAST 481/14 (search snippet; the PDF itself is blocked). — [ASVAB Process Guide 22FEB2016](https://www.uscg.mil/Portals/3/Documents/VOL_ED/ASVAB_Process_Guide_22FEB2016.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 32 figure is consistent across the Coast Guard's own site (as reported by two independent third parties and the task assignment), military.com, and test-ninjas; 36 and 40 on other pages are stale. The most defensible narrative: 40 was the long-standing floor (highest in DoD); it was lowered, with the current 32 floor associated with the Nov 2023 COMDTINST 1100.2H rewrite, and 1100.2I (June 2025) did not change it. The intermediate "36" step is asserted by one site with no date and should be flagged as unverified.
- On Tier 2: the site that gives a specific section number (1100.2I 4.B.7, 47 AFQT) is more precise than the one that says 50; the "50" may be a carry-over from DoD-wide convention or an older Coast Guard figure. Recommend the study tool present "Tier 2 (GED): higher minimum, reported as 47 under the 2025 instruction (some sources say 50); Tier 2 enlistments are limited and need Accessions Division approval; 15 semester hours of college credit converts a GED holder to Tier 1."

### Gaps
- Could not open COMDTINST 1100.2H or 1100.2I (media.defense.gov 403) to quote the AFQT table or confirm 47 vs 50 for Tier 2.
- No ALCOAST, MyCG article, or news release announcing the AFQT lowering (40 to 36 or to 32) was found in search; the Nov 2023 timing rests on a single third-party page.

## Key Question 4: Citizenship, education tiers, dependents, medical, fitness standards, swim

### Takeaway
Enlisted applicants may be U.S. citizens or lawful permanent residents; education is Tier 1 (diploma) or Tier 2 (GED, limited); dependents are capped (third-party: no more than three); medical is via MEPS; and the Coast Guard's current published recruit fitness standards are 29/15 push-ups (M/F) in one minute, a 1:18/1:09 forearm plank, and a 12:29/15:05 1.5-mile run, with swim training (not a published pass/fail swim entry standard) at boot camp.

### Cited Findings
- Fitness standards (official, from the static portion of the gocoastguard.com eligibility page, stated for "all new members, officer and enlisted"): push-ups in one minute males 29 / females 15; timed forearm plank males 1:18 / females 1:09; timed 1.5-mile run males 12:29 / females 15:05. — [gocoastguard.com eligibility requirements](https://www.gocoastguard.com/get-started/eligibility-requirements)
- Swim (official): "Both boot camp and OCS include swim training"; applicants uncomfortable in the water are advised to build swimming ability before arriving. — [gocoastguard.com eligibility requirements](https://www.gocoastguard.com/get-started/eligibility-requirements)
- Tattoos (official): "There's no limit on the number of tattoos you can have, but there are location restrictions and decency standards." — [gocoastguard.com eligibility requirements](https://www.gocoastguard.com/get-started/eligibility-requirements)
- Waivers (official): Coast Guard Recruiting Command can authorize "case-by-case waivers to certain entrance standards," and a recruiter determines whether any apply. — [gocoastguard.com eligibility requirements](https://www.gocoastguard.com/get-started/eligibility-requirements)
- 2025 PFT revision (official title/snippet only; page 403): MyCG, May 2025, "Coast Guard updates Boat Crew Physical Fitness Test" — the new requirements "also apply to any Coast Guard member using the Boat Crew PFT as the basis of their fitness tests, including new recruits entering boot camp." — [MyCG article 4181558](https://www.mycg.uscg.mil/News/Article/4181558/coast-guard-updates-boatcrew-physical-fitness-test/)
- Older (pre-plank) boot camp standards, dated: military.com page "Updated Nov 18, 2021" lists push-ups (60 s) 29 M / 15 F; sit-ups (60 s) 38 M / 32 F; 1.5-mile run 12:51 M / 15:26 F (printed "15.26"); swim circuit for both sexes: tread water 5 minutes, jump from a 5-foot platform, swim 100 meters. — [military.com Coast Guard Basic PFT](https://www.military.com/military-fitness/coast-guard-fitness-requirements/coast-guard-basic-pft) (third party, 2021; sit-ups and run times superseded by the plank/12:29/15:05 standards above)
- Another third-party guide (undated) describes a 6-foot platform jump and 100-meter swim. — [operationmilitarykids.org Coast Guard basic training guide](https://www.operationmilitarykids.org/coast-guard-basic-training-guide/) (search-snippet level; conflicts with military.com's 5-foot figure)
- Citizenship (third party): enlisted applicants must "Hold U.S. citizenship or be a legal permanent resident." — [military.com Join the Coast Guard](https://www.military.com/join-armed-forces/join-the-coast-guard); "Citizen of the United States or a Resident Alien." — [operationmilitarykids.org](https://www.operationmilitarykids.org/coast-guard-requirements/)
- Education (third party): "Have a high school diploma or equivalent." — [military.com](https://www.military.com/join-armed-forces/join-the-coast-guard); the Coast Guard "limits the number of Tier 2 (GED) applicants it accepts each year." — [operationmilitarykids.org](https://www.operationmilitarykids.org/coast-guard-requirements/); Tier definitions follow DoD criteria: Tier 1 = high school graduates (incl. college credit), Tier 2 = alternative credential holders such as GED, Tier 3 = non-graduates. — [test-ninjas.com GED page](https://test-ninjas.com/enlisting-with-a-ged-asvab)
- Dependents (third party): "Possess no more than three dependents." — [operationmilitarykids.org](https://www.operationmilitarykids.org/coast-guard-requirements/) (page updated Feb 2026 but carries other stale data, e.g., age 17-31; treat as unverified)
- Medical (third party): applicants must "Pass a military entrance medical exam (MEPS)"; disqualifying conditions require a waiver; the Coast Guard is described as "particularly strict" on asthma, ADHD, and certain mental-health diagnoses; waivers "not guaranteed." — [operationmilitarykids.org](https://www.operationmilitarykids.org/coast-guard-requirements/)
- Boot camp length/retest (third party): basic training is eight weeks at Cape May; recruits who fail the PFT get a second attempt in week seven. — [operationmilitarykids.org basic training guide](https://www.operationmilitarykids.org/coast-guard-basic-training-guide/) (search-snippet level)

### Inferences
- The plank replaced sit-ups and the run standard tightened (12:51 to 12:29 male; 15:26 to 15:05 female) between the 2021 third-party table and the current official page, consistent with the May 2025 Boat Crew PFT revision applying to recruits. A study tool should use the official 29/15, 1:18/1:09, 12:29/15:05 figures and date the sit-up/12:51 figures as pre-2025.
- The official page frames swimming as training rather than an entry test; the 5-ft platform / 100 m / 5-min tread figures are long-standing boot camp swim-circuit elements from third-party sources and should be labeled as such.

### Gaps
- Dependents cap and the medical standard reference (DoDI 6130.03) could not be confirmed from an official Coast Guard source in this environment.
- The official 2025 PFT article text (three specific changes) could not be read.

## Key Question 5: Can the AFQT minimum be waived?

### Takeaway
No. The Coast Guard's 2021 policy change expanded local waiver authority to composite scores and subtest minimums but explicitly excludes the AFQT; the enlistment AFQT floor is not among the waivable A-school score elements. The 2016 ASVAB/AFCT Waiver Request Procedures Process Guide (uscg.mil) is the pre-2021 reference but could not be opened from this environment.

### Cited Findings
- Waiver authority wording (official, via search snippet of the MyCG article, 22 Oct 2021): CO/OIC authority "is increased from five to 10 total points and now includes subtest minimums, other than the AFQT." — [MyCG, ASVAB score requirements changing for A schools](https://www.mycg.uscg.mil/News/Article/2816942/asvab-score-requirements-changing-for-a-schools/) (page 403 from here; text from search-engine snippet)
- PPC Flag Voice 563 (official, via snippet): "CO/OIC waiver authority increased from five to ten points, including subtest minimums." — [PPC news, Coast Guard ASVAB policy changes, Flag Voice 563](https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/ppc/news/Article/2816424/coast-guard-asvab-policy-changes-flag-voice-563/) (page 403 from here)
- Third-party restatement: the expanded authority "covers subtest minimums but not the AFQT"; Coast Guard Recruiting Command "can authorize waivers to some entrance standards case by case" but a qualifying AFQT "lets you apply; it is not an offer." — [test-ninjas.com](https://test-ninjas.com/coast-guard-jobs-by-asvab-score)
- The 2016 guide exists at the URL below (title "ASVAB Process Guide 22FEB2016"; it references ALCOAST 481/14) but returned 403. — [ASVAB_Process_Guide_22FEB2016.pdf](https://www.uscg.mil/Portals/3/Documents/VOL_ED/ASVAB_Process_Guide_22FEB2016.pdf)

### Inferences
- Combining the official snippet ("other than the AFQT") with the general gocoastguard.com statement that CGRC may waive "certain" entrance standards, the safe statement for a study tool is: composite/subtest requirements for a rating can be waived (10 points locally, more by the Rating Force Master Chief), but the AFQT enlistment minimum cannot; an applicant below the AFQT floor must retest.

### Gaps

## Key Question 6: A-school composite waiver policy (Nov 2021 change)

### Takeaway
Effective 1 November 2021, the Coast Guard lowered every A-school composite requirement by 10 points (diver excluded), raised CO/OIC waiver authority from 5 to 10 total points (now including subtest minimums other than AFQT), and made Rating Force Master Chiefs the waiver authority beyond 10 points; the service was already granting 300+ merit-based composite waivers a year.

### Cited Findings
- "All 'A' School ASVAB composite score requirements are adjusted by ten points (except DV)" / "The composite score requirements for all A-school (except diver) have been adjusted by 10 points. The specific subtests used to establish a minimum composite score for each rating have not changed." Effective November 1, 2021; article dated October 22, 2021. — [MyCG article 2816942](https://www.mycg.uscg.mil/News/Article/2816942/asvab-score-requirements-changing-for-a-schools/) (search-snippet text; page blocked)
- "That authority is increased from five to 10 total points and now includes subtest minimums, other than the AFQT." "RFMCs may grant waivers exceeding 10 points." "The Coast Guard already grants more than 300 merit-based composite score waivers annually for deserving members to attend 'A' schools." — [MyCG article 2816942](https://www.mycg.uscg.mil/News/Article/2816942/asvab-score-requirements-changing-for-a-schools/) (search-snippet text)
- PPC Flag Voice 563: "CO/OIC waiver authority increased from five to ten points, including subtest minimums." "RFMCs will now serve as their rating's 'A' School waiver authority for all waivers beyond CO/OIC authority." — [PPC, Coast Guard ASVAB policy changes, Flag Voice 563](https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/ppc/news/Article/2816424/coast-guard-asvab-policy-changes-flag-voice-563/) (search-snippet text; page blocked). The second PPC URL given in the assignment ([PPC article 2819883](https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/ppc/news/Article/2819883/)) also returned 403 and no snippet was obtained.
- Third-party restatement with added detail: since November 2021 recruiting accessions staff and unit COs can waive up to 10 total points; RFMCs handle larger composite waivers; a member refused a waiver "receives a Path to Success form"; the full A-school requirement list sits on a restricted SharePoint and some public school pages may still show pre-adjustment composites. — [test-ninjas.com](https://test-ninjas.com/coast-guard-jobs-by-asvab-score)
- No DVIDS reproduction of the article was found in search. — [DVIDS search attempt via web search](https://www.dvidshub.net/)

### Inferences
- Because the 10-point adjustment and the 10-point waiver authority stack, a member can effectively attend A-school up to 20 points below the pre-November-2021 composite with only local approval, and further with RFMC approval; AFQT remains the one unwaivable score element.

### Gaps
- Full MyCG/PPC article text, author, and the ALCOAST number implementing the change were not retrievable (403; no cache or DVIDS copy found).

## Key Question 7: ASVAB retest policy and PiCAT verification rules

### Takeaway
Retest waits are one calendar month after the initial test, another month before a second retest, then six months for any further retest; a 20+ point AFQT gain within six months triggers a confirmation test (C-Test). Scores are valid for enlistment for two years. PiCAT is a one-time, first-time-tester-only option that must be finished within 48 hours of starting and verified in person within 45 days; a successful verification makes the PiCAT scores the scores of record.

### Cited Findings
- "ASVAB retest policy requires a one-month wait between administration of the initial ASVAB and a retest." "To retake the ASVAB a second time, another one-month wait is required." "For any additional retests, a six-month wait is required between retests." "The retest policy is the same, regardless of whether the initial test is a student test or an enlistment test." — [officialasvab.com ASVAB Retest Policy](https://www.officialasvab.com/recruiters/asvab-retest-policy/)
- "Second retests administered before the one month period will be invalidated." After two retests, "The applicant may be administered a third retest six months from the date of the second retest." — [officialasvab.com ASVAB Retest Policy](https://www.officialasvab.com/recruiters/asvab-retest-policy/)
- Critical AFQT gain (20 or more points within 6 months): "the applicant is required to take a confirmation test (C-Test)"; afterward "The applicant may return for another standard retest six months from the date of the C-Test." — [officialasvab.com ASVAB Retest Policy](https://www.officialasvab.com/recruiters/asvab-retest-policy/)
- Invalidations: an initial test invalidated for administrative reasons "does NOT count as the first test"; a retest invalidated for administrative reasons "does NOT count"; if a test is invalidated for cheating, "The applicant must wait six months before retesting." — [officialasvab.com ASVAB Retest Policy](https://www.officialasvab.com/recruiters/asvab-retest-policy/)
- Applicant FAQ: "After you take your initial ASVAB, you must wait one calendar month to retake the test," another calendar month before a second retest, then six calendar months; "Your scores may be used for enlistment for up to two years from the date of testing." School-based (SST) scores require "a Verification Test (Vtest) ... before your scores can be used for enlistment," "provided that they can be verified as being yours." — [officialasvab.com Applicants FAQ](https://www.officialasvab.com/applicants/faq/)
- PiCAT: "Once an applicant begins taking the PiCAT, it must be completed within 48 hours." The access code "expires 30 days after it is issued." The verification test "must be taken within 45 days of when the PiCAT was taken" and "generally takes 25 to 30 minutes to complete." If verified, "the PiCAT scores will become the official ASVAB scores of record and can be used to determine enlistment eligibility." "PiCAT can be taken only once and is available only to those who have never taken the ASVAB." — [officialasvab.com PiCAT](https://www.officialasvab.com/recruiters/picat/)

### Inferences
- The one-month waits are calendar months (per the applicant FAQ wording), so the earliest third attempt is roughly two months after the initial test, and the fourth roughly eight months after.
- The PiCAT page does not state the consequence of a failed verification; standard practice (not confirmed here) is that the full-length verification/ASVAB then becomes the score of record and counts as the initial test for retest-wait purposes.

### Gaps
- The officialasvab.com PiCAT page does not say what happens if verification fails or whether a failed verification counts as the first test; not confirmed from an official source.
- No Coast Guard-specific retest (AFCT) policy text was retrievable (the 2016 uscg.mil guide is blocked).
