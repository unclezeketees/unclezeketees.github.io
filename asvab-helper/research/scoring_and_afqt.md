# ASVAB scoring mechanics, AFQT percentile, and the limits of estimating AFQT from practice tests

Scope note: "Official" below means officialasvab.com (DoD's ASVAB site), the Defense Manpower Data Center (DMDC) technical report by Segall (2004) that the site links as the 1997 score-scale document, the Office of People Analytics (OPA) executive note, DoD Population Representation reports, and U.S. Code. Everything from test-prep publishers or calculator sites is labelled THIRD PARTY.

Primary document used throughout: Daniel O. Segall, *Development and Evaluation of the 1997 ASVAB Score Scale*, Defense Manpower Data Center, July 2004 (DMDC Technical Report 2004-002), linked from officialasvab.com as the 1997 score scale PDF: https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf ("Segall 2004" below). Page numbers refer to the report's own pagination.

## Key question 1: Standard scores — mean, SD, reference population, norming

### Takeaway
Every ASVAB subtest is reported as a standard score that is a linear transformation of an IRT ability estimate (theta), defined so that the 1997 Profile of American Youth (PAY97) sample of roughly 6,000 18-to-23-year-olds has mean 50 and SD 10. Those norms were implemented in 2004 and, per OPA, have been re-evaluated and judged still representative; no newer national norming study was found.

### Cited Findings
- Official definition: "Subtest standard scores are defined as linearly transformed subtest scores θ̂. Unrounded standard scores are defined to have a mean of 50 and a standard deviation of 10 in the PAY97 sample" via S_k = 10[(θ̂ − μ̂)/σ̂] + 50, where μ̂ and σ̂ are the PAY97 sample mean and SD of theta for subtest k (eq. 2.1, p. 10) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- "In practice, standard scores are rounded to the nearest integer." Unlike the 1980 scale, the 1997 scale does NOT truncate standard scores at 20 or 80: "the range of standard scores is not truncated at 20 or 80 (three standard deviations from the mean)" because "an allowance for guessing is incorporated into the three parameter logistic scoring model, and because in instances of low precision, scores are appropriately regressed towards the mean by the use of a Bayesian prior" (p. 11 and footnote 2) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- Form 04D (the CAT-ASVAB reference form used in PAY97) slope/intercept parameters, Table 2.4 (p. 11): GS 11.543462 / 48.988873; AR 11.528721 / 48.365417; WK 11.032817 / 47.809880; PC 12.351821 / 45.886521; AI 14.183186 / 55.582509; SI 12.964407 / 55.952500; MK 10.025804 / 46.255061; MC 12.957792 / 51.247394; EI 11.034039 / 51.159592; AO 10.340850 / 50.273512 (S_k = A·θ̂ + B) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- officialasvab.com (counselors/educators Scores page): the subtest scale is set so that "the mean is set to 50 and the standard deviation is set to 10"; "ASVAB scoring is based on an Item Response Theory (IRT) model," specifically the three-parameter logistic (3PL) model (difficulty, discrimination, guessing); the final ability estimate is converted to a standard score through equating; "Equating studies are conducted for every CAT-ASVAB item pool" — [officialasvab.com, Scores (Counselors & Educators)](https://www.officialasvab.com/counselors-educators/scores/)
- Applicants page: "Standard Scores are scores that have a meaning relative to a national sample of youth aged 18 to 23." "About half the population scores at or above a Standard Score of 50" and "about 16% of the population scores at or above a Standard Score of 60." — [officialasvab.com, Scores (Applicants)](https://www.officialasvab.com/applicants/scores/)
- Norming sample: "The PAY97 consists of a nationally representative sample of about 6,000 American youths aged 18—23, with oversamples of Hispanic and Black youths. These youths were identified from a screening of over 90,000 housing units. In the summer and fall of 1997, the computerized adaptive testing version of the ASVAB was administered to study participants under standardized conditions." (Executive Summary, p. xi–xii) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- "The current national norms for the ASVAB were implemented in 2004," based on the Profile of American Youth 1997 (PAY97) study; "National norms are norms that are developed using a nationally representative sample of examinees." — [officialasvab.com, Norming Information](https://www.officialasvab.com/researchers/norming-information/)
- PAY97 details: "Conducted jointly by the Departments of Defense and Labor"; "The ETP sample included about 6,000 American youth age 18-23" (the prime enlistment pool as of 1 June 1997); a separate CEP sample of "some 4,700 students" in grades 10–12; participants were paid $75 to take the CAT-ASVAB at Sylvan testing centers; participation 77% (ETP) and 75% (CEP) — [officialasvab.com, 1997 Profile of American Youth](https://www.officialasvab.com/researchers/1997-profile-of-american-youth/)
- Why the scale was re-normed: on the 1980 scale "a score of 50 on the Armed Forces Qualification Test (AFQT) no longer represents the median score of the current youth population" (p. xi). PAY97 youth scored higher on the old scale (AFQT m = 52.3, s = 27.3) than PAY80 youth (m = 50.4, s = 28.9) (p. 9) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- Any newer norming? OPA footnote 1: "The relevance of existing norms is continually monitored. Recent evaluations indicate that the 1997 reference group and the 2004 norms are still representative of the current ASVAB population." — [OPA Executive Note, Appropriate Use of ASVAB Scores (PDF)](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/20260128_DTAC_ExecutiveNote_TO52-4.4.7-AppropriateUseofASVABScores.pdf)
- Score precision (official, FY2019 CAT applicants; P&P pooled FY2011–2019): reliability of AFQT 0.97 (CAT) / 0.94 (P&P); AR 0.91/0.87; WK 0.92/0.88; PC 0.86/0.74; MK 0.93/0.86. Standard error of measurement: AFQT 4.39 percentile points (CAT) / 5.87 (P&P); AR 2.61/3.02; WK 2.33/2.88; PC 2.88/3.82; MK 2.01/2.85 standard-score points. "approximately 68% of the applicants" have true scores within one SEM of their reported score — [officialasvab.com, Test Score Precision](https://www.officialasvab.com/researchers/test-score-precision/)

### Inferences
- Because standard scores are linear functions of an IRT theta (not of number correct), a practice tool that only knows "N right out of M" cannot reproduce an official standard score without item parameters and a scoring engine; the best it can do is approximate.
- Even the official CAT-ASVAB carries roughly ±4.4 AFQT percentile points of measurement error at one SEM, so any practice estimate should be presented as a band at least that wide, and realistically much wider.

### Gaps
- No official document found that announces any norming study more recent than PAY97 / the 2004 implementation; the only statement is OPA's that the norms were re-evaluated and remain representative (no date or report cited for that evaluation).

## Key question 2: Verbal Expression (VE) — how it is formed from WK and PC

### Takeaway
On the 1997 scale VE is NOT a simple sum of WK and PC. It is an optimally weighted composite of the unrounded WK and PC standard scores (PC weighted about 0.62 of WK), re-standardized to mean 50 / SD 10 in the PAY97 sample, and then rounded. The "add PC + WK number-correct and look up a table" method describes the obsolete 1980 scale.

### Cited Findings
- 1980-scale method (now obsolete): "For the ‘80 scale, the Verbal (VE) composite was formed from the standardized unit weighted sum X_WK + X_PC of number-right scores, where X_WK denotes the number-correct score on the 35-item WK test, and X_PC denotes the number-correct score on the 15-item PC test." (p. 12) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- 1997-scale method: "the VE composite on the ‘97 scale was formed from an optimally weighted composite of unrounded WK and PC standard scores. The optimal weights were specified from the least squares regression of the VE standard score (computed relative to the ‘80 metric) on S_WK and S_PC ... The ratio of the least-squares regression weights b_PC/b_WK = 0.61934 indicates that S_PC received about two-thirds the weight of S_WK ... the weighted composite S_WK + 0.61934 × S_PC was standardized to mean 50 and standard deviation 10." (p. 12) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- In theta terms (Form 04D): S_VE = (7.225587)·θ̂_WK + (5.010103)·θ̂_PC + 46.897156 (eq. 2.3, p. 12). "integer-valued counterparts ... S̆_VE and S̆_AS, will be used operationally. These are obtained by rounding S_VE and S_AS to the nearest integer." (p. 13) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- Rationale for weighting PC less: PC "is restricted by its shorter test length" and "this smaller weight for PC is desirable because of PC's relatively low reliability" (p. 12) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- officialasvab.com's public Scores pages do not mention VE at all; they say only that the AFQT "is computed using the Standard Scores from four ASVAB subtests: Arithmetic Reasoning (AR), Mathematics Knowledge (MK), Paragraph Comprehension (PC), and Word Knowledge (WK)" — [officialasvab.com, Scores (Applicants)](https://www.officialasvab.com/applicants/scores/); [officialasvab.com, Scores (Counselors & Educators)](https://www.officialasvab.com/counselors-educators/scores/)
- THIRD PARTY: several prep sites quote VE = 0.654918 × WK + 0.405617 × PC − 3.026712 (in standard-score units), attributing it to the 1997 DoD report, and say the simple sum "is out of date by nearly three decades" — [test-ninjas.com, ASVAB Verbal Expression](https://test-ninjas.com/asvab-verbal-expression) (via search summary; page not fetched directly)
- THIRD PARTY (obsolete method still published): For Dummies (Rod Powers) says the military "adds the number correct (1 point per correct answer)" on PC and WK and compares the total to a "Standard Scores (PC + WK)" lookup table (e.g., 50 correct → VE 62) — [dummies.com, A Detailed Look at Scoring the ASVAB](https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/study-skills-test-prep/armed-services/a-detailed-look-at-scoring-the-asvab-169209/)

### Inferences
- Algebra check (my derivation, not in the report): substituting θ̂_WK = (S_WK − 47.809880)/11.032817 and θ̂_PC = (S_PC − 45.886521)/12.351821 (Table 2.4) into eq. 2.3 gives S_VE = 0.654918·S_WK + 0.405617·S_PC − 3.026712, with weight ratio 0.61934, and S_WK = S_PC = 50 → S_VE = 50.000. So the third-party standard-score formula is exactly equivalent to Segall's eq. 2.3 for Form 04D. Because other forms are equated onto the same standard-score scale, this standard-score form of VE is the practical operational formula.
- The Dummies PC+WK number-correct table is the 1980-scale procedure that Segall explicitly describes as superseded; a tool that uses it is applying a method retired in 2004 (and, for paper forms, number-right scoring itself ended in January 2002 — see Q3).
- The VE formula only helps if you already have WK and PC *standard scores*; it does not by itself convert percent-correct into anything.

### Gaps
- No current official VE lookup table (standard scores → VE) is published on officialasvab.com; the formula above is the only official source, and it is in a 2004 technical report rather than on a public explainer page.

## Key question 3: AFQT — the 2VE + AR + MK formula, conversion to percentile, and whether raw-correct tables exist

### Takeaway
Officially, AFQT_S = S̆_AR + S̆_MK + 2·S̆_VE (sum of rounded standard scores, range roughly ≤109 to ≥269), and the percentile is the rounded cumulative percentage of the PAY97 sample at or below that composite, clamped to 1–99. DMDC published the full composite-to-percentile table (Segall 2004, Table 2.5, reproduced below). There is no official number-correct-to-standard-score table for the CAT-ASVAB because scoring is IRT-based and adaptive; since January 2002 even paper forms are IRT-scored.

### Cited Findings
- Formula: "The Armed Forces Qualification Test (AFQT) scores are calculated from a linear combination of math and verbal standardized subtest scores: AFQT_S = S̆_AR + S̆_MK + 2S̆_VE." Footnote 4: "This same formula was used to calculate AFQT scores on the ‘80 metric." (p. 13) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- Percentile definition: "The AFQT scores are reported on a percentile metric, where AFQT% represents the percentage of applicants scoring at or below the given score AFQT_S": AFQT%(AFQT_S) = Rnd[100 · Σ_{x=1}^{AFQT_S} f(x)], "where f(x) is the discrete probability density function for the composite distribution AFQT_S in the ‘97 sample." "Rounded percentile scores of zero were set equal to 1; rounded percentile scores of 100 were set equal to 99." (eq. 2.5, p. 13) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- Official composite → percentile conversion, Table 2.5 "‘97 AFQT Percentile Conversion" (pp. 14–15), AFQT_S → AFQT%:
  - ≤109→1; 110–118→2; 119–124→3; 125–133→4; 134–137→5; 138–141→6; 142–145→7; 146–147→8; 148–151→9; 152–153→10; 154–156→11; 157→12; 158–159→13; 160→14; 161–162→15; 163–164→16; 165–166→17; 167→18; 168–169→19; 170→20; 171→21; 172–173→22; 174→23; 175→24; 176–177→25; 178→26; 179→27; 180→28; 181→29; 182→30; 183→31; 184→32; 185→33; 186→34; 187–188→35; 189→36; 190→38 (no composite maps to 37); 191→39; 192→40; 193→41; 194→42; 195→43; 196→44; 197→45; 198→46; 199→47; 200→48; 201→49; 202→50; 203→51; 204→52; 205→53; 206→54; 207→55; 208→56; 209→57; 210→59 (no 58); 211→60; 212→61; 213→62; 214→63; 215→64; 216→66 (no 65); 217→67; 218→68; 219→69; 220–221→70; 222→71; 223→72; 224→73; 225→74; 226→75; 227→76; 228→77; 229→78; 230→79; 231→80; 232→81; 233–234→82; 235→83; 236→84; 237–238→85; 239→86; 240→87; 241–242→88; 243→89; 244–245→90; 246→91; 247–248→92; 249–251→93; 252–253→94; 254–256→95; 257–259→96; 260–263→97; 264–268→98; ≥269→99 — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
  - Selected cumulative percentages from the same table: 200 → 47.80% cumulative → 48; 202 → 49.77% → 50; 183 → 30.95% → 31; 152 → 9.91% → 10; 249 → 92.66% → 93 — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- Public pages: "AFQT scores are reported as percentiles between 1-99"; a percentile "indicates the percentage of examinees in a reference group that scored at or below that particular score"; reference group is "a sample of 18 to 23 year old youth who took the ASVAB as part of a national norming study conducted in 1997"; e.g., "an AFQT score of 90 indicates that the examinee scored as well as or better than 90% of the nationally-representative sample." The public pages give no formula and no conversion table. — [officialasvab.com, Scores (Applicants)](https://www.officialasvab.com/applicants/scores/); [officialasvab.com, Scores (Counselors & Educators)](https://www.officialasvab.com/counselors-educators/scores/)
- CAT-ASVAB scoring (official): "every examinee is assigned an initial ability estimate of θ = 0.0"; after each item a sequential Bayesian procedure updates the estimate; the final estimate is the Bayesian modal estimate (mode of the posterior). The final ability estimate is converted to a standard score through equating; "Equating studies are conducted for every CAT-ASVAB item pool (and for every paper-and-pencil ASVAB form)." For paper tests, "any unanswered items are treated as incorrect." The 3PL model is used for final ability estimates on both P&P and CAT. — [officialasvab.com, ASVAB Scoring (Researchers)](https://www.officialasvab.com/researchers/scores/)
- CAT-ASVAB adaptive mechanics: "The first item administered in the CAT-ASVAB is of average difficulty"; after each response "the item best suited for the examinee's estimated ability level is selected to be administered next"; "if an examinee answers an item incorrectly on the CAT-ASVAB, an easier item is selected to be administered next." Scored item counts: GS 15, AR 15, WK 15, PC 10, MK 15, EI 15, AI 10, SI 10, MC 15, AO 15 (plus up to 15 unscored tryout items each). Time limits without tryout items (min): GS 12, AR 55, WK 9, PC 27, MK 31, EI 10, AI 7, SI 6, MC 22, AO 18. Incomplete tests: "A penalty procedure is applied to all examinees who do not complete the test before time runs out ... scoring the items that were not completed as though they were answered at random"; "In almost all cases it is not necessary to apply the penalty." — [officialasvab.com, The CAT-ASVAB](https://www.officialasvab.com/researchers/cat-asvab/)
- Number-right scoring is gone even for paper forms: "Beginning in January 2002, however, a new scoring method, based on item response theory (IRT), was implemented for P&P-ASVAB forms 25A, 25B, 26A, and 26B ... the form-specific equatings associated with the previous number-right-based forms became unnecessary, since in the context of IRT, the ability estimates are interchangeable across different sets of items." Footnote: "This IRT-based method assigns scores based on the mode of the posterior density function." (pp. 23–24) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- CAT forms were historically equated form-by-form (reasons include that "CAT-ASVAB has shorter test lengths than P&P-ASVAB, so a handful of poorly specified item response functions (IRFs) might have a large effect on score distributions"); for the 1997 scale "CAT-ASVAB forms were equated to the reference form using a linear method which matches the first two moments of the distribution of the estimated ability parameters θ̂" (p. 24) — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- THIRD PARTY confirmation of the above: a prep site states that because the computer version is adaptive and IRT-scored, no fixed number-correct conversion exists — reported in search results for [easy-prep.org ASVAB scores](https://easy-prep.org/blog/asvab-scores) (page not fetched directly); Dummies notes that on the CAT "harder questions are worth more points than easier questions" and "AR and MK questions are worth between 1 and 3 points each" — [dummies.com](https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/study-skills-test-prep/armed-services/a-detailed-look-at-scoring-the-asvab-169209/) (the "1–3 points" claim is the publisher's simplification; it does not appear in any official source I found)

### Inferences
- There IS an official composite→percentile table (Table 2.5), so the step "sum of standard scores → AFQT percentile" can be done exactly by a practice tool. The missing link is the step before it: raw practice-test performance → subtest standard scores, which officially requires IRT theta estimates and form-specific equating that no public document supplies.
- Table 2.5 skips percentiles 37, 58, and 65 (no integer composite rounds to them); the AFQT's lumpiness at the composite level (each composite point ≈ 0.5–1.4 percentile points mid-range, far less at the tails) means a 1-point change in one subtest's standard score moves the AFQT by about 1–3 percentile points near the middle but by almost nothing at the extremes.
- Confirmed: no official raw-correct → standard score table exists for the CAT-ASVAB, and the officialasvab.com statement that paper forms are also 3PL-scored (with blanks = wrong) plus Segall's January 2002 note means none exists for current paper forms either. The only official number-correct method on record is the 1980-scale VE sum, which is superseded.

### Gaps
- Table 2.5 was computed on Form 04D in 2004; whether DoD has refreshed the composite→percentile mapping as new CAT item pools are equated is not stated anywhere I could find (equating is designed to keep the standard-score scale fixed, so the table should still apply, but no official statement confirms "still in use" as of 2026).
- officialasvab.com does not publish a table of standard scores vs. number of items correct for any form, and none was found on DTIC via search within the call budget.

## Key question 4: AFQT categories (I through V, with IIIA/IIIB and IVA/IVB/IVC)

### Takeaway
officialasvab.com publishes the eight-band category table (I, II, IIIA, IIIB, IVA, IVB, IVC, V); DoD policy documents collapse IVA–IVC into "Category IV" (10–30). The same bands appear in Segall 2004 with their nominal population percentages.

### Cited Findings
- Official table (identical on both public Scores pages):
  | AFQT Category | Percentile range |
  |---|---|
  | I | 93–99 |
  | II | 65–92 |
  | IIIA | 50–64 |
  | IIIB | 31–49 |
  | IVA | 21–30 |
  | IVB | 16–20 |
  | IVC | 10–15 |
  | V | 1–9 |
  — [officialasvab.com, Scores (Counselors & Educators)](https://www.officialasvab.com/counselors-educators/scores/); [officialasvab.com, Scores (Applicants)](https://www.officialasvab.com/applicants/scores/)
- The Enlistment Eligibility page uses the six-band version (I 93–99; II 65–92; IIIA 50–64; IIIB 31–49; IV 10–30; V 1–9) and states "Applicants that score in AFQT category IIIA or higher may qualify for enlistment incentives" and "The minimum AFQT score required to qualify for enlistment varies across the different branches of service" (no branch minimums are listed; it refers readers to airforce.com, goarmy.com, uscg.mil, marines.com, navy.com) — [officialasvab.com, Enlistment Eligibility](https://www.officialasvab.com/applicants/enlistment-eligibility/)
- Nominal (definitional) percentages of the population in each band, Table 2.2 (p. 9): I 7.5%; II 28%; IIIA 15%; IIIB 19%; IVA 10%; IVB 5%; IVC 6%; V 9.5% — [Segall 2004](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/1997score_scale.pdf)
- OPA: "Percentile score ranges are attributed to one of six AFQT categories for reporting purposes." "the IIIA category is used to identify applicants for enlistment incentives." — [OPA Executive Note (PDF)](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/20260128_DTAC_ExecutiveNote_TO52-4.4.7-AppropriateUseofASVABScores.pdf)
- THIRD PARTY (Mometrix) reproduces the same eight-band table — [mometrix.com, ASVAB Scores](https://www.mometrix.com/blog/asvab-scores/)

### Inferences
- A practice tool can safely display the eight-band table verbatim with attribution to officialasvab.com; it should avoid implying that any band other than the service minimum "passes."

### Gaps
- None material.

## Key question 5: Practical estimation — what test-prep publishers say, published approximate tables, and the OPA "Appropriate Use" note

### Takeaway
No credible publisher claims an accurate percent-correct → AFQT conversion. The honest third-party consensus (Union Test Prep, JobCannon, Honest MOS) is that it is "nearly impossible" or at best a model-based band with no real norm group; the only published "tables" (Dummies) use the retired 1980 number-right method. DoD's own 20-item AFQT Predictor Test comes with the disclaimer that its prediction "is not a guarantee." OPA's note says the only validated uses of ASVAB scores are selection, classification, and career exploration, and warns against any non-validated use.

### Cited Findings
**Official**
- DoD's own predictor: the AFQT Predictor Test (APT) is unproctored, online, adaptive, 20 items drawn from AR, MK, WK, and PC, takes 20–30 minutes, and is designed to "help predict an applicant's AFQT score if they were to take the ASVAB." Applicants do not see their score; only the recruiter who registered them can. Disclaimer: "an applicant's score on the APT is not a guarantee of what the applicant will score on the ASVAB," and there will likely be random errors associated with such predictions. — [officialasvab.com (APT page, ?p=3755)](https://www.officialasvab.com/?p=3755)
- OPA Executive Note "Appropriate Use of Armed Services Vocational Aptitude Battery (ASVAB) Scores" (header reads "March 2020 EXECUTIVE NOTE 2025-189"; the file is dated 20260128 and cites OPA 2025 papers and the "Department of War," so this is a revised 2025/2026 edition of a 2020 note). Key points:
  - "There are three validated and approved uses for ASVAB scores": (1) Selection (AFQT), (2) Classification (Service composites), (3) Career Exploration (CEP composites). "The purpose of this information paper is to elaborate on these three approved uses and strongly advise against any alternative uses for ASVAB scores."
  - Validity "is not a property of a test"; "Each new proposed use must undergo its own validation. That is, a test that is meaningful for one objective (e.g., military selection) may be less meaningful (or even misleading) in another context."
  - "The AFQT is a composite score calculated using the Standard Scores from four ASVAB subtests: AR, MK, PC, and WK. AFQT scores are reported as percentiles from 1–99." Reference group: "a sample of 18- to 23-year-old youth who took the ASVAB as part of a national norming study conducted in 1997 (Segall, 2004)."
  - Standard scores: "About half the population score at or above a standard score of 50, and about 16% score at or above 60."
  - DoDI 1145.01 benchmarks: "the AFQT category benchmark for accessions within a given fiscal year is 60 percent with AFQT percentile scores of 50 (AFQT Category IIIA) or better. Also, no more than 4 percent of an accession cohort with AFQT percentile scores between 10 and 30 (AFQT Category IV) may be enlisted, and no one with a score below 10 (AFQT Category V) may be inducted or enlisted."
  - Score validity period: DoDI 1304.12E "specifies that ASVAB scores are considered valid for enlistment purposes for a maximum of two years from the date of ASVAB administration."
  - Non-applicant (student) scores "may not reflect their best efforts" because many retake after graduation; OPA has no independent measure of motivation.
  - Inappropriate uses listed: evaluating educational outcomes; predicting ACT/SAT (and vice versa: "it is a misuse of ACT/SAT scores to predict ASVAB scores ... validity evidence to support such uses does not exist"); postsecondary admissions/scholarships. "the Office of People Analytics does not endorse the use of ASVAB scores for purposes other than those for which the test was designed."
  - Minimum-score guidance to states: "because the minimum score fluctuates based on various Service requirements and other factors, states should check with the military services for the most up-to-date information. The ASVAB CEP does not make recommendations for minimum ASVAB score requirements."
  — [OPA Executive Note (PDF)](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/20260128_DTAC_ExecutiveNote_TO52-4.4.7-AppropriateUseofASVABScores.pdf)

**THIRD PARTY — publishers and calculator sites**
- Union Test Prep: ASVAB scoring "involves a complicated conversion system and isn't the straightforward percentage scoring you've encountered in school," so "it's nearly impossible to convert an ASVAB practice test score to an AFQT or Composite score." — [uniontestprep.com, ASVAB Practice Test](https://uniontestprep.com/asvab/practice-test) (quoted from search-result summary; direct fetch returned HTTP 403)
- JobCannon (publishes an estimate and its method): "The AFQT composite is 2 × VE + AR + MK, so verbal counts twice"; it weights verbal 50%, AR 25%, MK 25% of percent-correct; "A weighted share of 25 percent, which is guessing level on four-option questions, maps to a percentile of 1 ... A perfect paper maps to 99. Everything in between is on the straight line joining them"; example points 50% → 34, 62.5% → 50, 90% → 86; result shown as a ten-point band. It takes *standard scores* as input, not percent correct, and does not label its result an estimate. — [test-ninjas.com, AFQT Score Calculator](https://test-ninjas.com/afqt-score-calculator)

### Inferences
- The only defensible pipeline for a practice tool is: (a) if the user enters official *standard scores*, compute VE with the Segall weights, sum 2VE+AR+MK, and look up Table 2.5 — this part is exact and official; (b) if the tool only has percent-correct on its own items, any mapping to a percentile is a model, not a measurement, because the items are not calibrated to the ASVAB item bank, there is no IRT theta, and there is no norm group. It should be labelled an unofficial estimate and shown as a wide band.
- OPA's validation argument applies directly: estimating an AFQT from an unofficial practice test is an unvalidated use of the AFQT scale, so a tool that reports "your AFQT is 67" without heavy qualification is making a claim DoD would not endorse. Reporting percent-correct by subtest, plus the official category table and official minimums, avoids that problem.
- Reasonable floor on uncertainty: even the real CAT-ASVAB has SEM ≈ 4.4 AFQT points; a 15–20-item-per-subtest fixed-form practice test with uncalibrated items will have substantially more error than that, so a ±10-point band (as JobCannon's ten-point bands imply) is a minimum, not a conservative, choice.
- The Dummies-style PC+WK table should not be used: it is the 1980 method, and Segall documents that the 1997 scale deliberately changed the weighting.

### Gaps
- No publisher (Kaplan, Peterson's, Barron's, Mometrix) was found to publish a validated percent-correct → AFQT table with error bounds; Barron's materials were not located online within the call budget.
- No empirical study was found comparing any commercial practice test's predicted AFQT to actual AFQT outcomes.
- The APT's own prediction accuracy (e.g., correlation with later AFQT) is not published on the page found.

## Key question 6: Statutory floor (10 U.S.C. § 520) and the Tier 1 / Tier 2 education distinction

### Takeaway
10 U.S.C. § 520 caps the share of recruits scoring in the 10th–30th percentile band (4% since a 2023 amendment, waivable by the Secretary of Defense up to 20%) and bars enlisting non-high-school graduates below the 31st percentile; the absolute "no one below the 10th percentile" rule is stated in DoDI 1145.01 as quoted by OPA. DoD sorts education credentials into Tier 1 (regular high school diploma or higher), Tier 2 (GED and alternative credentials), and Tier 3 (no credential); Tier 2 applicants face higher AFQT minimums set by each service. The Coast Guard's own site states an AFQT minimum of 32; the Tier 2 figure (reported as 47) could only be sourced to third parties.

### Cited Findings
- 10 U.S.C. § 520(a)(1): the number of persons originally enlisted or inducted into an armed force in a fiscal year whose AFQT score is "at or above the tenth percentile and below the thirty-first percentile" may not exceed 4 percent of all persons originally enlisted or inducted into that force that year. (a)(2): on request of the Secretary concerned, the Secretary of Defense may raise the limit to no more than 20 percent and must notify the Armed Services Committees within 30 days. A 2023 amendment (Pub. L. 118–31) lowered the base cap from 20 to 4 percent. — [Cornell LII, 10 U.S.C. § 520](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/520)
- § 520(b): a person who is not a high school graduate may not be enlisted unless the AFQT score is "at or above the thirty-first percentile," but a person cannot be denied enlistment solely for lacking a diploma if the enlistment is needed to meet established strength requirements. The section text does not mention GED or home-school diplomas; a 1998 pilot (Pub. L. 105–261 § 571) directed DoD to treat GED and home-school recipients as high school graduates from Oct 1, 1998 to Sep 30, 2003. — [Cornell LII, 10 U.S.C. § 520](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/520)
- Per the LII text as summarized, § 520 "does not set a separate floor below the 10th percentile. The 10th percentile is the lower bound of the band that the 4 percent cap covers." — [Cornell LII, 10 U.S.C. § 520](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/520)
- The Category V bar is stated as DoD policy: per DoDI 1145.01, "no more than 4 percent of an accession cohort with AFQT percentile scores between 10 and 30 (AFQT Category IV) may be enlisted, and no one with a score below 10 (AFQT Category V) may be inducted or enlisted"; the quality benchmark is 60 percent at Category IIIA or better. — [OPA Executive Note (PDF)](https://www.officialasvab.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/20260128_DTAC_ExecutiveNote_TO52-4.4.7-AppropriateUseofASVABScores.pdf), citing [DoDI 1145.01](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/114501p.pdf)
- Future Servicemember Preparatory Course (Pub. L. 118–31 § 546, as amended): if covered non-prior-service enlistees exceed 10 percent of originally enlisted personnel, the service must establish a course; non-prior-service enlistees scoring below the 31st percentile must enroll before basic training; sunsets Sep 30, 2028. — [Cornell LII, 10 U.S.C. § 520 (notes)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/520)
- Official Tier labels (DoD Population Representation report, FY2000, Coast Guard chapter): "Tier 1: Regular High School Graduate or Higher"; "Tier 2: GED, Alternative Credentials"; "Tier 3: No Credentials"; "College Experience (Part of Tier 1)". — [DoD Population Representation FY2000, ch. 7 (CNA mirror)](https://cna.org/pop-rep/2000/html/chapter7/c7_np_education.htm)
- DoDI 1145.01 (per search summaries of the instruction's glossary): Tier 2 (medium priority) includes alternative credential holders; Tier 3 (low priority) includes non-graduates with AFQT at or above the 31st percentile; the education benchmark is 90 percent Tier 1 — [DoDI 1145.01 (esd.whs.mil PDF)](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/114501p.pdf) (direct fetch returned HTTP 403/503 at every mirror; wording is from search-result excerpts, not verified against the document)
- Coast Guard official minimum: "The minimum ASVAB AFQT score for enlisting in the U.S. Coast Guard is 32." "Officer programs have different standardized test requirements." No diploma-vs-GED distinction is stated on that page. — [gocoastguard.com, Get Started](https://www.gocoastguard.com/get-started)
- THIRD PARTY on the Coast Guard Tier 2 minimum: prep sites report AFQT 47 for Tier II (GED/alternative credential) applicants, attributing it to COMDTINST M1100.2I (Coast Guard Recruiting Manual, June 2025) section 4.B.7, with every Tier II enlistment also requiring Accessions Division approval, and placing 15 semester hours / 20 quarter hours of college credit in Tier I (Table 4-5). Other sites say 50 (sometimes "with 15 college credits") or 40 for diploma holders; the figures conflict. — [test-ninjas.com, Enlisting with a GED](https://test-ninjas.com/enlisting-with-a-ged-asvab); [test-ninjas.com, Coast Guard jobs by ASVAB score](https://test-ninjas.com/coast-guard-jobs-by-asvab-score); [uniontestprep.com, Minimum AFQT by branch](https://uniontestprep.com/asvab/blog/minimum-asvab-afqt-score-for-each-branch-of-the-military) (all via search summaries)
- THIRD PARTY (Kaplan) on other services: 31 for Army and Marines; 50 for GED holders — [kaptest.com, What is the AFQT?](https://www.kaptest.com/study/asvab/what-is-the-afqt/) (via search summary)
- Context on the Category IV cap being enforced: a DoD Inspector General report (Dec 2025) found the Army and Navy miscounted low-scoring recruits, with the Navy at 2,763 (11.3%) of 24,388 FY2025 enlistments in Category IV against the 4 percent limit — [USNI News, Dec 19 2025](https://news.usni.org/2025/12/19/dod-ig-army-navy-miscounted-recruits-with-low-academic-scores); [DoDIG-2026-031 (PDF)](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Dec/15/2003842682/-1/-1/1/DODIG-2026-031%20FSPC%20GRADUATES_REDACTED_SECURE.PDF) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- "Statutory floor at the 10th percentile" is the common shorthand, but the verified statutory text regulates the 10–30 band and the 31st-percentile rule for non-graduates; the categorical ban on Category V is documented in DoD policy (DoDI 1145.01). A practice tool should phrase it as "below the 10th percentile (Category V) is ineligible under DoD policy; the 10–30 band is tightly capped by statute."
- For a Coast Guard–focused tool, the safe official statement is "AFQT 32 minimum (gocoastguard.com); GED/alternative-credential (Tier 2) applicants need a higher score and extra approval; confirm the current figure with a recruiter." Hard-coding 47 would rest on third-party reports only.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve DoDI 1145.01 itself (all mirrors returned 403/503), so the glossary definitions of Tier 1/2/3 and the 90% Tier 1 benchmark are cited only through OPA's quotation and search excerpts.
- Could not retrieve COMDTINST M1100.2I (Coast Guard Recruiting Manual) or any gocoastguard.com page stating the Tier 2 AFQT minimum; the 47 figure is unverified.
- The current (post-2023) full text of § 520 was read through a fetch summary of the LII page rather than the raw statute; the 4%/20% structure is consistent with the OPA note and IG reporting, but exact wording beyond the quoted fragments was not captured.
