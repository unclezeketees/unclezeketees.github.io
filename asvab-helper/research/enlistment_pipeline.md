## Key Question 1: Timeline — ASVAB timing, score validity, DEP length/rules, wait from signing to ship, 2024-2026 recruiting climate

### Takeaway
ASVAB scores are good for two years, so a junior can test in spring/summer of junior year and still enlist the summer after graduation; the Coast Guard's DEP allows up to 365 days between signing and shipping (military.com), and because the service over-recruited in FY2025 (5,204 enlisted vs. 4,300 goal, highest since 1991) seats at Cape May are in demand, with the service paying $5,000-$10,000 "Quick-Ship" bonuses to those who take the next open boot camp class. No official Coast Guard average wait-time figure was found.

- [OFFICIAL] Retest rules: wait one calendar month after initial test before retesting; an additional calendar month before a second retest; thereafter six calendar months between retests — [officialasvab.com FAQ](https://www.officialasvab.com/applicants/faq/)

### Inferences
- Practical timeline for a current junior: take the production ASVAB with a recruiter (or ensure a school CEP ASVAB is MEPS-proctored/verifiable) in spring of junior year or summer before senior year; scores stay valid through graduation plus a year. Expect 1-6 months from prescreen submission to a signed DEP contract depending on medical complexity (MEPCOM 74-day average), then a DEP of up to 12 months with the ship date set to after high school graduation.
- With FY2025 enlistments at 121% of goal, the constraint in 2025-2026 is likely boot camp seats rather than recruiter interest; the "ship expeditiously" bonus (up to $10,000) signals the service is paying people to fill near-term open classes, so flexible applicants may ship faster and earn money, while those locked to a post-graduation date may wait longer.

## Key Question 2: Recruiter meeting — documents to bring, prescreen questions, honesty

### Takeaway
The official site says to gather detailed documentation and submit a thorough medical history before MEPS; the Coast Guard explicitly screens criminal and credit history. Since MHS Genesis reached all MEPS in 2022, omitted medical history is routinely discovered from civilian records, so recruiters and reporting agree honesty on the prescreen is now effectively mandatory.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] gocoastguard.com tells applicants to "collect detailed documentation" and keep "important documents organized and accessible," to submit a detailed medical history before MEPS is scheduled, and that the recruiter checks criminal background and credit — [gocoastguard.com Get Started](https://gocoastguard.com/get-started)

- [THIRD PARTY] military.com: 17-year-olds need parental consent to enlist — [military.com CG recruiting FAQ, Sep 28 2026](https://www.military.com/join-armed-forces/coast-guard-recruiting-faqs.html); [operationmilitarykids.org How to Join](https://www.operationmilitarykids.org/how-to-join-the-coast-guard/) (same)

### Inferences
- A sensible recruiter-meeting packet for a 17-year-old: certified birth certificate, Social Security card, state ID/driver's license, high school transcript (and diploma at graduation), parent/guardian available to sign consent, list of every doctor/clinic visit, prescription history and any surgeries/diagnoses (with records), and any court/police paperwork. (Specific CG document list is inferred from the official Cape May ID requirements, the official "detailed medical history" requirement, and standard practice; the CG Recruiting Manual was not fetched.)
- Because MEPS now pulls civilian records through MHS Genesis, a prescreen answer that conflicts with records can delay or end an application; disclosing childhood ADHD medication or inhaler use up front and pursuing a waiver is the realistic path.

## Key Question 3: MEPS — medical exam, disqualifying/waiverable conditions, DoDI 6130.03 summary, MHS Genesis

### Takeaway
MEPS applies DoD Instruction 6130.03 Vol. 1 (asthma after age 13, uncorrectable vision, hearing thresholds, many surgeries, etc.); the Coast Guard Recruiting Command can grant case-by-case waivers, and a 2025 DoD review/pilot has loosened ADHD and asthma waiver requirements, though the specifics for the Coast Guard (a DHS service that follows DoD accession medical standards at MEPS) were not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] "You must pass a complete medical exam and drug test at MEPS"; detailed medical history must be submitted for MEPS review before scheduling — [gocoastguard.com Get Started](https://gocoastguard.com/get-started)
- [OFFICIAL] "Coast Guard Recruiting Command can authorize case-by-case waivers to certain entrance standards for otherwise strong candidates. Your recruiter decides whether any apply." — [gocoastguard.com Eligibility Requirements](https://www.gocoastguard.com/get-started/eligibility-requirements)
- [OFFICIAL] DoDI 6130.03 Vol. 1 "Medical Standards for Military Service: Appointment, Enlistment, or Induction" is the governing instruction; Change 4 dated Nov 16, 2022 — [esd.whs.mil DoDI 6130.03 Vol 1 (via Navy mirror)](https://www.med.navy.mil/Portals/62/Documents/NMFSC/NMOTC/NAMI/ARWG/Miscellaneous/Medical%20Standards%20for%20Military%20Service%20613003_vol01.pdf); Vol. 2 (retention) — [esd.whs.mil 613003_vol2](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/613003_vol2.PDF)
- [THIRD PARTY, citing DoDI 6130.03 Vol 1 and a DoD standards update dated Feb 3, 2026] military.com (updated July 9, 2026) summary of disqualifying conditions, described as "red flags, not automatic disqualifiers," with waivers possible — [military.com Medical Conditions That Can Keep You from Joining](https://www.military.com/join-armed-forces/disqualifiers-medical-conditions.html):
  - Vision: distant vision not correctable to at least 20/40 in each eye; near vision not correctable to 20/40 in the better eye; refractive error beyond +/-8.00 diopters spherical equivalent or astigmatism beyond 3.00 diopters; any condition requiring contact lenses for adequate correction; keratoconus, glaucoma history, uveitis history, lens implants. Color vision standards are set by each service.
  - Asthma/airway (after 13th birthday): history of asthma, reactive airway disease, exercise-induced bronchospasm or asthmatic bronchitis; symptoms (cough, wheeze, chest tightness, shortness of breath, exercise limitation); or prescription/use of airway medication (inhaled or oral steroids, leukotriene antagonists, beta agonists).
  - Hearing: thresholds exceeding 25 dB average at 500/1000/2000 Hz; 30 dB at any of 500/1000/2000 Hz; 35 dB at 3000 Hz; 45 dB at 4000 Hz; unexplained asymmetry of 30 dB or more; hearing-aid history; implanted hearing device, cholesteatoma or middle/inner-ear surgery; Meniere's, recurrent labyrinthitis or vertigo in prior 12 months.
  - Surgeries: any thoracic surgery; any bariatric surgery; any joint replacement/resurfacing; hip arthroscopy/FAI; spinal fusion; knee ligament reconstruction within 12 months; incisional corneal surgery or certain laser refractive surgery with timing/stability requirements; peptic ulcer or pancreatic surgery; heart valve repair/replacement; abdominal surgery within prior 3 months; urogenital reconstruction/gender-affirming surgery with an 18-month wait.
  - Metabolic syndrome counts only with three of five criteria, one of which is waist circumference over 35 in (women)/40 in (men).
- [NEWS] April 2025: DoD announced a review of the disqualifying-conditions list; a pilot program expanded to 51 conditions including ADHD and childhood asthma; the pilot "eliminated the waiver requirement for individuals previously diagnosed with ADHD if they did not receive medical treatment for the condition for one year, down from three, and for those with asthma if they haven't used an emergency inhaler" — [military.com, Apr 29 2025](https://www.military.com/daily-news/2025/04/29/defense-department-review-list-of-medical-conditions-disqualify-potential-recruits-serving.html); [MOAA summary, 2025](https://www.moaa.org/content/publications-and-media/news-articles/2025-news-articles/recommended-reads/dod-to-review-list-of-disqualifying-medical-conditions/)
- [THIRD PARTY] Conflicting ADHD rule statements across secondary sources: "ADHD medication use within the previous 24 months is disqualifying" vs. "disqualifying if still medicated or recent history" — [operationmilitarykids.org Medical Waiver Guide](https://www.operationmilitarykids.org/military-medical-waiver-guide/) and [military.com disqualifiers](https://www.military.com/join-armed-forces/disqualifiers-medical-conditions.html) (the DoDI text itself was not read; see Gaps)
- [OFFICIAL] Army accessions research (FY24 AMSARA report) exists on waiver outcomes — [WRAIR FY24 AMSARA Special Report](https://wrair.health.mil/Portals/87/Documents/FY24_AMSARA_Special_Report_Updated15Oct.pdf) (not read)
- [NEWS] MHS Genesis: see Key Question 2 findings — [Military Times 2023](https://www.militarytimes.com/news/your-military/2023/04/10/the-genesis-of-todays-recruiting-crisis/); [Army.mil 2024](https://www.army.mil/article/274844)
- [OFFICIAL] GAO: in FY2024, 657 recruits (15%) were discharged from Cape May — [GAO-25-107224](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107224/index.html) (reasons not broken out in the text read)

### Inferences
- For a 17-year-old, the most common MEPS flags are likely childhood asthma/inhaler use after 13, ADHD medication, eyesight/color vision, and weight; each is reviewable, and the 2025 DoD pilot shortened the ADHD medication-free window to one year for some services. ## Key Question 4: Tattoos, piercings, dependents, citizenship, age — Coast Guard-specific

### Takeaway
Coast Guard policy (COMDTINST 1000.1F, May 2024) allows unlimited tattoos below the collar with hand tattoos up to 2.5 inches on the back of each hand, one finger tattoo, one tattoo behind each ear, no palm/thumb tattoos and nothing visible above the ODU crew-neck T-shirt collar. Enlisted applicants must be U.S. citizens or lawful permanent residents aged 17 (with parental consent) to the low 40s; single parents may enlist with a waiver and dependent-care plan since November 2022.

## Key Question 5: Coast Guard boot camp — location, length, phases, fitness test, swim, reversion/discharge, graduation

### Takeaway
Boot camp is an eight-week course at Training Center Cape May, NJ (the only enlisted basic training site), with classes starting most weeks; the official entry fitness benchmarks are 29/15 push-ups, 1:18/1:09 forearm plank and 12:29/15:05 1.5-mile run (male/female), and the official site confirms swim training but publishes no swim standards. GAO reports 15% (657) of FY2024 recruits were discharged from Cape May; reversion (being set back to a junior company) for fitness, academic or performance failures is described only by third-party sources.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] "The Coast Guard's only enlisted basic training is held at the Coast Guard Training Center in Cape May, NJ." "Recruit basic training is an eight-week course of instruction with new classes starting most weeks." — [gocoastguard.com Basic Training](https://gocoastguard.com/get-started/basic-training)

## Key Question 6: After boot camp — non-rate assignment vs. direct A-school, Direct Access requests, typical waits

### Takeaway
Most recruits graduate as non-rates (Seaman/Fireman E-2/E-3) and report to a unit, then apply for A-school through Direct Access (since April 2023); as of April 2026 the Coast Guard waived the 4-month A-school wait for critical ratings and, starting June 1, 2026, will place non-rates with 12 months at a unit onto A-school waitlists involuntarily based on ASVAB and service needs. Guaranteed A-school out of boot camp is available for high-demand ratings when ASVAB scores qualify.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] "Your first assignment is as an unspecialized seaman or fireman. After settling into your first unit, you can request advanced specialty training." — [gocoastguard.com Basic Training](https://gocoastguard.com/get-started/basic-training)
- [OFFICIAL] Guaranteed specialty training incentive: "If your ASVAB scores qualify for a high-demand specialty, you may be guaranteed that school right after boot camp"; advanced paygrade to E-3 after boot camp for college credits, JROTC/ROTC, police/sea explorer programs or scouting achievements; buddy program; guaranteed first-assignment District in certain areas; hometown recruiting leave of up to a few weeks after boot camp — [gocoastguard.com Hiring Incentives](https://www.gocoastguard.com/hiring-incentives)
- [OFFICIAL] ALCOAST 134/23 (April 2023): Class A-school requests "shall be submitted through Direct Access"; members may see request status at any time — [ALCOAST 134/23](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCG/bulletins/352fc63); step-by-step PPC guides: [SS MBR A-School Request Submission (Feb 2024)](https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/Portals/10/CG-1/PPC/GUIDES/GP/SelfService/Member/SS%20MBR%20-%20A-School%20Request%20Submission.pdf) and [Self-Service Submitting an A-School Request](https://www.dcms.uscg.mil/Portals/10/CG-1/PPC/GUIDES/GP/SelfService/Member/Self-Service%20-%20Submitting%20an%20A-School%20Request.pdf)
- [OFFICIAL] ALCOAST 106/26 (Apr 27, 2026, RADM C. E. Fosse): 1,200 non-rate positions added; about 800 eligible non-rates had not applied for A-school; >1,300 E4/E5 vacancies; the 4-month A-school wait is waived for all critical ratings (not listed); starting June 1, 2026, non-rates at their unit 12 months without selecting an A-school may be placed on a waitlist by EPM based on ASVAB scores and service needs; members with <24 months left who do not intend to continue may file a Career Intentions Worksheet; process to be reassessed end of Assignment Year 2027; contact HQS-SMB-CGPSC-EPM-ASCHOOLREQUESTS@USCG.MIL — [ALCOAST 106/26](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCG/bulletins/414c94c)
- [OFFICIAL] ALCGENL 092/24: "Non-rate return to unit beta test" for DC, EM and MK ratings (A-school graduates returning to prior units) — [ALCGENL 092/24](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCG/bulletins/38eb553) (not read in full)
- [OFFICIAL] Cyber Mission Specialist formal A-school waitlist opened Feb 3, 2025; members may enter at E-3 or E-2 — [MyCG, 2025](https://www.mycg.uscg.mil/News/Article/4029861/coast-guard-announces-formal-a-school-for-cyber-mission-specialists)
- [THIRD PARTY] 30 months remaining on enlistment is required to enter Class A school; may need to extend; A-school lasts "from a few months to more than two years," paid, technical rather than disciplinary — [military.com CG recruiting FAQ, Sep 28 2026](https://www.military.com/join-armed-forces/coast-guard-recruiting-faqs.html)
- [OFFICIAL] GAO FY2024 bonus table shows a separate "A school bonus (no prior service) $20,000-$75,000" vs. "Non-rate/prior service bonus $2,000," confirming two distinct enlistment paths (guaranteed A-school contract vs. non-rate) — [GAO-25-107224](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107224/index.html)

### Inferences
- In 2026 the Coast Guard is actively pushing non-rates into A-schools (waived 4-month wait for critical ratings, involuntary waitlisting after 12 months at unit), so a new graduate who scores well on the ASVAB and applies promptly through Direct Access should face shorter waits than in prior years, particularly for critical ratings; the 4-month minimum wait still applies for non-critical ratings.
- The guaranteed-A-school contract from the recruiter (with the large bonus) is the only way to skip non-rate time; otherwise expect at least 12 months as a non-rate plus the waitlist.

### Gaps
- No published list of 2026 "critical ratings" and no official average A-school waitlist durations by rating were found; ALCOAST 106/26 directs members to a CG-internal SharePoint guide.
- Non-rate tour length norms (commonly described as 1-2 years) were not confirmed by an official source.

## Key Question 7: Enlistment contract length options and the current bonus program ("up to $75,000")

### Takeaway
gocoastguard.com itemizes bonuses as up to $60,000 for a critical rating, up to $15,000 for college credits, up to $10,000 to ship expeditiously and up to $15,000 for Reserve assignments, capped by statute at $75,000 per enlistee (GAO); GAO's FY2024 table lists A-school bonuses of $20,000-$75,000 by program. No official page listing which ratings currently qualify or the required contract lengths was found; contract-length options are not stated on gocoastguard.com.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL] "Get up to a $75,000 Bonus When you enlist." — [gocoastguard.com FAQ](https://www.gocoastguard.com/faq) (links to /hiring-incentives)
- [OFFICIAL] Signing bonuses (enlisted), "authorized based on current recruiting needs," may apply to all recruits, to enlistments in certain time periods, or require qualifying skills/certifications/education: up to $60,000 to affiliate with a critical rating; up to $15,000 for higher-education credits; up to $10,000 to ship expeditiously; up to $15,000 for Reserve assignments. The page says incentive programs "change frequently" and does not state contract length, effective dates, the list of critical ratings, or whether bonuses stack — [gocoastguard.com Hiring Incentives](https://www.gocoastguard.com/hiring-incentives)
- [OFFICIAL] GAO FY2024 bonus table: A-school bonus (no prior service) $20,000-$75,000 varying by program; critical skills bonus (prior service) $20,000-$65,000; non-rate/prior service bonus $2,000; Quick-Ship bonus $5,000-$10,000; college credit bonus $3,000-$15,000 depending on credits; statutory cap $75,000 per enlistee — [GAO-25-107224, May 14 2025](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107224/index.html)
- [THIRD PARTY] 30 months remaining on enlistment required for A-school entry — [military.com CG recruiting FAQ](https://www.military.com/join-armed-forces/coast-guard-recruiting-faqs.html)
- [THIRD PARTY] General (not CG-specific): standard enlistment is four years active plus four in the Inactive Ready Reserve for an eight-year total military service obligation — [usmilitary.com how to join](https://usmilitary.com/how-to-join-the-us-military/) (low-quality aggregate source; included only to flag the common 8-year MSO)

### Inferences
- The "$75,000" headline is the statutory maximum (10 U.S.C. enlistment bonus cap as cited by GAO), reached only by a guaranteed-A-school contract in a critical rating with stacked college-credit/quick-ship bonuses; a typical 17-year-old non-rate enlistee without college credits would see at most the quick-ship bonus (up to $10,000) unless they contract for a critical rating.
- Bonus payout structure (lump sum vs. installments, repayment on failure to complete A-school) is standard in DoD programs but was not found for the CG.

### Gaps
- No official source for Coast Guard enlistment contract options (e.g., 4-year vs. 6-year active, Reserve obligation) — gocoastguard.com and the fetched FAQ do not state them; the Recruiting Manual was not retrieved.
- No current (FY2026) list of bonus-eligible ratings; ALCOAST bonus messages (typically "ALCGRECRUITING"/"ALCGENL" enlistment bonus bulletins) were not located in this search.
- Whether bonuses apply to 17-year-olds in DEP identically to adults was not addressed by any source.
